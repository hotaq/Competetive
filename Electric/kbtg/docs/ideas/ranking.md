## g1 (score 9.2)

**Strengths:** Best-in-class problem understanding: 'เงินเดือนหมดก่อนสิ้นเดือน' is the segment's #1 pain, verified against the team's own research, and the Thai-market whitespace (zero predictive cash-flow features across all six competitors) is well established. Purest Track-2 fit: first-party data → predictive model → better decision, no new UI paradigm, no security control, advice-only (lowest compliance burden of all five ideas). DS application is exemplary: quantile regression with pinball loss, honest 80% intervals, pay-cycle-aligned rolling-origin backtesting, cold-start cohort curves, Monte-Carlo runway simulation, seasonal-naive benchmarking — the eval design itself is the differentiator. Feasibility is high: every input is data K PLUS already holds; the demo plan is realistic in 48h with a genuinely killer money moment (what-if chips moving the runway number live, driven by real model inference). Persona insight (loss aversion, concrete near-dates, forecast-as-weather) is sharp and design-driving.

**Weaknesses:** Fraud/scam coverage is only a second-order, hard-to-attribute effect (honestly flagged, but judges scoring the dual-goal challenge may dock it). Closed-world blind spot (e-wallet/other-bank spend) systematically overstates runway. Forecast trust is fragile — one confident early miss ('boy who cried wolf') can kill adoption, and mitigation is partial. Several load-bearing Thai facts still need pre-pitch verification.

## g2 (score 8.3)

**Strengths:** Excellent problem framing of the knowing–doing gap (Thai literacy above OECD average yet weak saving behavior) with a precise mechanism: intervene at the moment money arrives, sized by forecast, not rule-of-thumb. Strong DS stack: quantile-forecast sizing (sweep only what survives the pessimistic q10 path), GMM soft segmentation, uplift/Qini targeting with sleeping-dog suppression, tiered cold-start with shadow-mode validation, causal A/B with guardrails. 'Why this amount' transparency screen is a regulator- and judge-winning artifact. Fails safe by construction (conservative quantile, auto-refund, one-tap undo). Segment-matched framing and streak resilience show genuine behavioral-design maturity.

**Weaknesses:** Demo impact is softer than g1 — an automatic sweep is harder to make visceral than a live what-if number, and the causal savings lift cannot be shown in a hackathon (12-week readout admitted as post-hackathon). Auto-moving a customer's money carries the highest trust/PDPA/ops burden of the money-management ideas; one wrong sweep destroys the product. Uplift layer needs experiment data that won't exist at launch (admitted, but it means the 'learning system' is partly roadmap, not demo). Depends on the same forecast engine g1 builds, so it's effectively g1's successor feature rather than an independent bet.

## g4 (score 7.7)

**Strengths:** Best-sourced problem statement of all five ideas (real cited URLs: Nation Thailand victim-age and ฿52k average-loss data, BOT 2023 rules) — evidence quality judges can check. Impressive DS breadth: supervised deviation scoring + unsupervised per-user baselines + graph features (fan-in, pass-through, dormancy-burst, node2vec/GraphSAGE) + Thai entity-resolution + isotonic calibration with an explicit false-positive-per-user-month alert-fatigue budget — the trade-off literacy that separates practitioners from slideware. Clever segment insight: First Jobbers' salary-anchored baselines make personal deviation unusually high-signal, and the savings-trajectory-break feature links fraud to money management. Dramatic demo (scripted scam timeline → live scored interruption → entity-mismatch reveal). Explicitly stays on the permitted data-driven fraud lens (behavioral scoring, anomaly detection — never a block).

**Weaknesses:** Strategic track-fit risk: the challenge states money management/saving is the CORE and fraud may be covered 'only through' data lenses — a fraud-first pitch where saving is incidental could be judged as missing the core mandate; the 'scam prevention IS money management' argument is clever but a gamble. Label lifeline (AOC 1441 victim-case data, TBA mule-list fields) is speculative and unverified, and the supervised legs degrade without it. Interbank graph blindness weakens the counterparty scorer — precisely the scam transfers that leave KBank rails. <300ms real-time scoring at confirm-screen is a hard engineering bar; adversarial drift is acknowledged but unsolved.

## g3 (score 7.4)

**Strengths:** Strong, under-served problem: BNPL fragmentation blindness is real and rising among Thai First Jobbers, with a credible behavioral anchor (first-miss as the highest-leverage intervention moment, shame as the help-seeking barrier). Nice DS progression (rules/dictionary → XGBoost recurrence features → Thai transformer on noisy descriptors) and a genuinely supervised, label-rich task (delinquency prediction with PR-AUC, calibration, lead-time metrics). The deterministic payoff-order optimizer makes the model decision-useful, and the DSR-preview at new-leg origination is a superb pre-commitment moment. NCB enrichment correctly scoped as consent-gated Phase-2 — good data-scoping discipline.

**Weaknesses:** The core premise is structurally undermined by the closed-world problem: K PLUS sees only its own payment legs, so 'see everything you owe' is really 'see the subset paid through K PLUS' — and BNPL is precisely the debt most likely to be auto-debited from other rails/wallets. Shame-sensitive topic means false-positive obligations are doubly costly, and opt-out is a fragile guardrail. Payoff optimizer is not ML (fine, but reduces the DS surface). Zero fraud-lens coverage. Segment fit is narrower — only indebted First Jobbers, not the whole cohort.

## g5 (score 6.3)

**Strengths:** Most sophisticated pure-DS architecture in the set: GraphSAGE inductive embeddings (elegant cold-start answer), LightGBM on graph+behavioral features, PU-learning honesty about label survivorship bias, two-model uplift targeting, and a four-phase behavioral model (susceptibility → onset → transition → confirmation) that demonstrates deep problem mechanics. The 'remove the motive, not just the mechanism' Green tier is a genuinely original prevention insight linking stress relief to recruitment resistance, and the limitations slide reads like a bank model-risk team wrote it.

**Weaknesses:** Poorest track fit: the challenge centers money management and victimization; this targets potential *perpetrators*, and only the Green tier touches saving. The headline stat is internally suspicious (Jan 2025 → Mar 2026 growth span looks like a data error — must be fixed or dropped). Label access (shared mule lists, field-level scope) is speculative; survivorship bias in labels is admitted but unresolved. False-positive harm is uniquely severe — telling a young user they resemble a criminal is reputational dynamite for a bank. Rare-event synthetic-only demo ('caught 11 days before' on fabricated labels) will not convince skeptical judges; hardest build of all five in the time available.

## Recommendation

Pitch g1 (Money Runway) as the headline idea. It is the only proposal that scores 8+ on all five judging axes: it attacks the segment's #1 verified pain with the challenge's core mandate (money management), applies the most methodologically honest data science in the set (quantile forecasting with pinball loss, calibrated intervals, pay-cycle-aligned backtesting, explicit cold-start cohorts, causal A/B design), is the most feasible as an actual K PLUS feature (advice-only, built entirely on data the bank already holds, minimal PDPA burden), and has the single best hackathon money moment — the live what-if simulator moving the runway number in real time, driven by real model inference rather than mocked numbers. Fix before pitch: pull verified Thai scam-loss and PDPA citations, and prepare the closed-world (e-wallet blindness) disclosure as a deliberate honesty slide. Keep g2 (Safe-to-Save Autopilot) as the back-pocket runner-up: it is the strongest long-term product and shares g1's forecast engine, so if judges probe 'so what does the user DO with the forecast?', pivot to g2's payday auto-sweep sized by the same quantile model — positioning g1 as the intelligence foundation and g2 as the action layer makes the team look like it has a roadmap, not just a demo.
